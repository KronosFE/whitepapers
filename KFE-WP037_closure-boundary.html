The Closure Boundary: Helium-3 Fraction At Least 0.15

There is a sharp fuel-mix threshold below which the burner cannot close &mdash; and it is published.

The burner closes only when the helium-3 fraction of the fuel is at least 0.15. Below that, the charged-power budget cannot fund the end losses; at and above it, net electricity becomes reachable. The boundary is a computed threshold, stated as one.

The science

Sweeping the helium-3 fraction, the engineering gain crosses unity at x_He3 = 0.15, reaching Q_E 1.191 with a positive net output at that point. The threshold exists because raising the helium-3 fraction lifts the charged-power share and cuts the neutron fraction together — both feed the same budget.

Why it matters

A published closure boundary tells a fuel planner exactly how helium-3-rich the mix must be, and tells a physicist exactly which mixes to test. It is the fuel-side companion to the plug-ratio requirement on the confinement side.
