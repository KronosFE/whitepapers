Low-Neutron by Design

Tuning the Neutron Output From the Fuel Line

The burner's neutron production is set decades in advance, at the fuel mix &mdash; and it is adjustable.

How many neutrons the burner makes is chosen at the fuel line. Raising the helium-3 fraction from 0.20 to 0.30 cuts neutron output about 1.75x for almost no loss in gain, which is exactly why the reference mix sits at 0.30. Neutron output is a knob, and the design turned it down.

The science

Neutrons in the burner come from D-D side reactions and the secondary D-T burn. Fewer deuterium-deuterium encounters means fewer neutrons, so a helium-3-richer mix is a quieter mix. From 0.20 to 0.30, the neutron fraction falls from 7.2% to 4.1%; pushing to 0.50 reaches 1.5% at a 13% gain cost.

Why it matters

A designer who can trade neutron output against gain and fuel demand can place the machine wherever the mission needs it. The published trade curve makes that a transparent engineering choice, not a hidden assumption.
